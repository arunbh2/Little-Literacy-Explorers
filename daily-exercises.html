Daily Exercises for Reading & Writing

Week 1: Phonological Awareness

These initial exercises develop the ability to hear and manipulate sounds in spoken language, the foundation for reading success.

Day 1

Sound Hunt

Materials Needed:
  • Small basket or container
  • 5-6 small objects from around your home

Duration: 15 minutes

15:00

Objective

Help your child develop awareness of beginning sounds in words by identifying objects that start with the same sound.

Steps

1

Select a target sound for today's exercise (for example, the /s/ sound as in "snake").

2

Go on a "sound hunt" around your home with your child, looking for objects that begin with the target sound. For /s/, you might find a sock, spoon, soup can, salt shaker, etc.

3

As you find each object, clearly emphasize the beginning sound: "Ssssock starts with /s/."

4

Place each found item in your basket.

5

Once you've collected 5-6 items, sit together and review each one, again emphasizing the beginning sound.

6

Extension: Add 2-3 items that begin with a different sound and ask your child to identify which ones don't belong in the /s/ collection.

Tips for Success

  • Focus on the sound, not the letter name (say /s/ as in "snake," not the letter name "ess").
  • Keep the activity playful and congratulate all attempts.
  • If your child struggles to identify the sound, simply model it clearly and continue without correction.
Day 2

Rhyme Time

Materials Needed:
  • Collection of simple rhyming objects or picture cards
  • Optional: Simple rhyming book

Duration: 15 minutes

15:00

Objective

Develop your child's ability to recognize and produce rhyming words, a key phonological awareness skill.

Steps

1

Start with a simple rhyming game. Say, "I'm thinking of a word that rhymes with 'cat'..." and let your child guess (hat, bat, sat, etc.).

2

Play with 3-4 different rhyming word families (e.g., cat/hat, dog/log, fish/dish).

3

Lay out your rhyming objects or picture cards and ask your child to match the pairs that rhyme.

4

Read a simple rhyming book together, emphasizing the rhyming words. Pause before reading the second rhyming word to see if your child can predict it.

5

Create silly rhymes with your child's name: "Hannah, banana, fanna, danna..."

Tips for Success

  • If your child is just beginning to understand rhyming, focus on recognition before asking them to produce rhymes.
  • Emphasize the rhyming portions of words by slightly exaggerating them.
  • Incorporate rhyming throughout your day: "Time for your snack on the black mat!"
Day 3

Syllable Clapping

Materials Needed:
  • Pictures of familiar objects with 1-3 syllables
  • Optional: Small drum or claves

Duration: 15 minutes

15:00

Objective

Help your child recognize that words can be broken into smaller parts (syllables), building awareness of word structure.

Steps

1

Explain that words are made up of parts called syllables, and we can hear these parts by clapping them.

2

Demonstrate with your child's name, clapping once for each syllable (e.g., "Em-i-ly" = 3 claps).

3

Practice with family members' names, clapping the syllables together.

4

Show a picture or name an object, then clap its syllables together (e.g., "pen-cil" = 2 claps).

5

Have your child sort pictures or objects by their number of syllables: one-syllable pile, two-syllable pile, etc.

6

Play "I Spy" with syllables: "I spy something with 2 syllables" (ta-ble, pen-cil, ap-ple).

Tips for Success

  • Start with familiar words that have clear syllable breaks.
  • If clapping is challenging, try tapping knees or using a drum.
  • Make it playful by exaggerating the mouth movements for each syllable.
Day 4

Initial Sound Isolation

Materials Needed:
  • Small basket or tray
  • Collection of small objects with different beginning sounds

Duration: 15 minutes

15:00

Objective

Develop your child's ability to isolate and identify the initial sound in words.

Steps

1

Place 5-6 small objects on a tray (e.g., ball, cup, dog figure, fish, pen).

2

Name each object, emphasizing the first sound: "/b/-ball," "/c/-cup," etc.

3

Ask your child to find an object that begins with a specific sound: "Can you find something that starts with /d/?"

4

Continue with each object, having your child identify the beginning sound.

5

Play "I Spy" with initial sounds: "I spy with my little eye something that begins with /b/."

6

For an added challenge, ask your child to find objects around the room that begin with a specific sound.

Tips for Success

  • Focus on the pure sound, not the letter name (say /b/ as in "ball," not the letter name "bee").
  • Start with sounds that are easier to isolate like /s/, /m/, /b/ rather than blends.
  • If your child struggles, model the answer and try again with a different object.

Week 2: Letter-Sound Correspondence

These exercises help your child connect the sounds they hear with the letters that represent them.

Day 5

Sandpaper Letters: Introduction

Materials Needed:
  • Homemade sandpaper letters (a, m, s, t) or textured letters
  • Small objects beginning with these sounds
  • Tray or mat

Duration: 15 minutes

15:00

Objective

Introduce the first set of letter-sound correspondences using multiple senses (visual, auditory, tactile).

Steps

1

Create a peaceful setting with minimal distractions.

2

Introduce one letter at a time, starting with 'a': "This is the letter that makes the /a/ sound as in apple."

3

Trace the letter with your index and middle fingers, following proper letter formation (demonstrate the correct direction).

4

Say the sound clearly: "/a/" (not the letter name "ay").

5

Invite your child to trace the letter while saying the sound.

6

Place objects beginning with /a/ next to the letter.

7

Repeat with 'm', 's', and 't' if your child shows interest and engagement.

Tips for Success

  • Follow the three-period lesson: 1) This is /a/. 2) Show me /a/. 3) What sound is this?
  • Focus on just 1-2 letters in this session if your child is new to this.
  • DIY sandpaper letters: Cut letters from sandpaper and glue to card stock.
  • Alternative: Write letters in glue on cardboard and sprinkle with sand.
