Arin at the Beach

5 minute read Beginning Reader
Short 'a' Sounds CVC Words Beach Turtle

Arin had a hat. It was a tan hat with a band.

Arin ran to the sand. The sand was hot on his feet.

"I will tap the sand with my hand," said Arin.

Arin sat on the sand and dug with his hand.

He dug and dug in the sand.

Arin had a map. The map was in his bag.

"I will find the gap in the rocks," said Arin.

Arin ran to the rocks. He saw a gap.

In the gap, Arin saw a small pad.

It was not a pad! It was a turtle on the sand!

"Hi," said the turtle. "I am Tom."

"I am Arin," said Arin. "Can you swim?"

"Yes, I can swim," said Tom. "I can swim fast."

"I can swim too," said Arin. "But not as fast as you."

Tom and Arin sat on the sand. They saw the big waves.

"Let's swim!" said Tom.

Arin and Tom had fun in the sea.

The sun was hot. Arin put on his hat.

"I must go back," said Arin.

"I will see you next time at the beach," said Tom.

Arin waved at Tom. He had a new friend at the beach.

The Tall Giraffe

5 minute read Beginning Reader
Long 'a' Sounds Silent 'e' Giraffe

Arin went for a walk. It was a sunny day.

He made his way to the big field. He could play there.

On the way, Arin came to a gate. The gate was open.

Arin went through the gate. He saw a tree with fruit.

The fruit was up high. Arin could not take it.

"I wish I could reach that fruit," said Arin.

Then Arin heard a soft sound. He looked up.

A tall giraffe stood by the tree. Its neck was very long.

"Hello," said the giraffe. "My name is Jane."

"Hello, Jane," said Arin. "My name is Arin."

"Do you like this fruit?" asked Jane.

"Yes, but it is too high for me," said Arin.

Jane stretched her long neck. She could reach the fruit.

Jane took some fruit with her mouth. She gave it to Arin.

"Thank you, Jane!" said Arin. He was happy.

The fruit tasted sweet. Arin ate it all up.

"Would you like to play?" asked Arin.

"Yes, I would like that," said Jane.

Arin and Jane played all day long.

When it was time to go home, Arin waved to Jane.

"I will come back another day," said Arin.

Jane nodded her head. She was happy to have a new friend.

Big Blue Whale

6 minute read Intermediate Reader
Long 'u' Sounds Diphthongs Whale Ocean

Arin stood on the pier. He looked out at the blue ocean.

The water sparkled in the sunlight. It was a beautiful view.

"I wonder what lives under the sea," said Arin.

Just then, Arin saw something big in the water. It made a huge splash!

A large blue whale came up from below. It was truly enormous!

The whale sprayed water from its spout.

"Hello down there!" called a deep voice.

Arin looked up. The whale was talking to him!

"Hello," said Arin. "Who are you?"

"My name is Blue," said the whale. "I am a blue whale."

"I'm Arin," said the boy. "I've never met a whale before."

"Most people haven't," said Blue. "We usually stay deep in the ocean."

"Why did you come up here?" asked Arin.

"I heard you wondering about what lives under the sea," said Blue. "I thought I could tell you."

"Oh, please do!" exclaimed Arin with excitement.

Blue began to describe the magical world under the waves.

"There are fish of every hue," said Blue. "Some glue themselves to rocks. Some cruise through the water like me."

"We have gardens of coral that grow like trees. There are valleys so deep that the sun never reaches them."

"That sounds amazing!" said Arin. "I wish I could see it."

"Maybe one day you will," said Blue. "Humans have special suits and boats that let them explore under the sea."

"When I grow up, I want to explore the ocean," said Arin.

"That's a good dream to have," said Blue. "But remember, the ocean is vast. Even I haven't seen all of it."

"Thank you for telling me about your home," said Arin.

"You're welcome," said Blue. "I must go now. My pod is waiting for me."

With another huge splash, Blue dove back under the waves.

Arin waved goodbye. He had learned so much from his new friend.

As he walked home, Arin dreamed of the blue depths and all the wonders that must be hiding there.

How to Use These Stories

Reading Together

  • Create a calm, comfortable reading environment.
  • Use the "Show Phonetic Highlights" feature to emphasize the target sounds.
  • Read slowly and clearly, emphasizing the highlighted sounds.
  • After reading, discuss the story and ask simple comprehension questions.
  • Encourage your child to point out words with the target sound.

Supporting Emerging Readers

  • Start with the beginning reader stories marked with the appropriate tag.
  • Use the "finger under the word" technique to track reading.
  • Let your child read the words they know and assist with others.
  • Celebrate successful decoding of new words.
  • Use the "Print Story" feature to create a physical copy for practice.
